# Translation of the Document to English

This document is a discussion paper released by the UK non-profit organization Article 36 in November 2017, aimed at promoting public oversight of weapon development and use. The document mainly explores the development of directed energy weapons (DEW), potential risks, and related legal and regulatory issues 1.

## Overview of Directed Energy Weapons

Directed energy weapons are defined as systems that produce "a beam of concentrated electromagnetic energy or atomic or subatomic particles" to incapacitate, injure, kill, or destroy personnel or objects 2. This definition does not include weapons that use sound waves or ultrasound to affect targets 3.

The document points out that directed energy weapons have evolved from theoretical concepts to the critical point of technological maturity 4. Currently, directed energy weapons mainly come in three forms:

- **Laser weapons**: Capable of shooting down aircraft and missiles, or using intense light to "dazzle" or disorient people 5.
- **Electromagnetic wave weapons**: Using electromagnetic waves of other wavelengths such as millimeter waves or microwaves, which can be used to attack human or hardware targets 6.
- **Particle beam weapons**: Using particle beams to destroy or damage the molecular or atomic structure of targets 7.

## Types and Technologies of Weapons

### Laser Weapons

Laser weapons were first theoretically proposed by Albert Einstein in 1917 8. Over the decades, the technology has been widely applied in commercial and military fields, such as metal cutting, welding, target designation, and ranging 9. The document categorizes the military laser technologies under development into three types:

- **Chemical lasers**: Use toxic chemical mixtures as fuel, require complex logistical support, and come with significant environmental and health risks 10.
- **Electric and solid-state lasers**: More stable and easier to transport, but inefficient because most of the energy is lost as heat when generating the beam 11.
- **Free electron lasers**: Generate megawatt-level laser beams by passing an electron stream through an alternating magnetic field 12. This type of laser avoids issues with chemical fuels and heat generation but is extremely bulky 13.

It is noteworthy that in 1995, laser weapons intended to cause permanent blindness were prohibited under Protocol IV of the Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons (CCW) before they were widely used 14. However, countries continue to develop laser systems for countering military hardware and electronic sensors, as well as "dazzlers" that can temporarily "dazzle" or disorient people 15.

### Microwave and Millimeter Wave Radiation Technologies

Multiple militaries are weaponizing microwave and millimeter wave radiation technologies 16. These weapons incapacitate military hardware by bombarding electronic systems with energy pulses, causing them to overload and shut down 17. Reports indicate that China, Russia, and the United States are all actively developing this technology 18.

Millimeter wave weapons can be used for crowd control and dispersal, operating on the principle of heating the skin to an intolerable pain temperature to attack people 19. For example, the United States' "Active Denial System" can produce a strong burning sensation on the skin without leaving visible marks 20.

### Particle Beam Weapons

Particle beam weapons are more similar to traditional kinetic weapons; they destroy the molecular or atomic structure of targets by emitting atomic or subatomic particles 21. Their effect is similar to lightning, rapidly heating the target material until it explodes 22.

The document points out that although particle accelerator technology has been used in scientific research and medicine, particle beam weapons have not been widely developed due to technical challenges, such as the lack of portable weapon-grade accelerators 23. In Earth's atmosphere, they require enormous power supplies, while in space, they need extremely precise control of beam characteristics 24.

## Risks and Legal Challenges

The document emphasizes that the development and use of directed energy weapons raise multifaceted concerns 25:

- **Health and Safety**: Exposure to directed energy weapons may cause short-term and long-term physical and psychological harm 26. For example, lasers may cause skin burns or blindness 27; millimeter wave weapons can penetrate clothing, causing intense skin pain and severe burns 28; particle beam weapons may cause severe burns and other injuries consistent with ionizing radiation 29.
- **Legal Ambiguity**: Certain directed energy weapons may circumvent existing legal restrictions, such as the ban on blinding laser weapons 30. Their non-kinetic injury mechanisms may challenge interpretations of traditional protective principles, such as prohibiting "superfluous injury or unnecessary suffering" 31.
- **Environmental Impact**: There is very little public data on the environmental and health effects of directed energy weapons, with significant uncertainty 32. In particular, chemical lasers use toxic chemical mixtures that pose serious environmental hazards if accidents occur or they are abandoned 33.
- **Blurring the Boundaries Between Law Enforcement and Warfare**: Some directed energy weapons are promoted for multiple contexts and purposes, which may further blur the boundaries between law enforcement and warfare, traditionally governed by different normative systems 34.
- **Accountability Issues**: Certain directed energy weapons, such as millimeter wave weapons, are designed to act covertly and silently, which may make harmful actions easy to conceal and raise concerns about accountability and effective remedies for victims 35.

## Governance and Regulatory Recommendations

The document calls on the States Parties to the Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons to take action to address the challenges posed by directed energy weapons 36:

- **Ongoing Monitoring**: Monitor the research and development of directed energy weapons and assess their potential to challenge existing weapon restrictions and bans 37.
- **Legal Assessment**: Ensure respect for the letter and spirit of the Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons and its protocols, and evaluate whether new injury mechanisms comply with the principle of prohibiting "superfluous injury or unnecessary suffering" 38.
- **Reaffirmation and Evaluation**: Reaffirm the ban on blinding laser weapons and evaluate whether Protocol IV of the Convention provides sufficient protection 39.
- **Increasing Transparency**: Encourage transparency and integrate considerations of directed energy weapons into ongoing work, ensuring a preventive approach 40.

The document concludes that although there is still debate over whether directed energy weapon systems have become an imminent reality, their development requires careful and comprehensive scrutiny to understand their potential humanitarian and other impacts 41. Currently, no existing international mechanism is actively considering this issue 42.
